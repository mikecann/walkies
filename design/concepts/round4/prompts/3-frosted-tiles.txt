Concept art for "Walkies", an iPhone app that turns dog walks into exploring your own suburb. The hidden parts of the suburb are revealed tile by tile as you walk. This image is one of several style directions, all showing the same screen so they can be compared. The aim is calm and refined, not cartoony.

Attached images:
1. A sheet of the app's collectible secret stickers. This sticker art is only for secrets (bits of local history found on walks). Do not draw the interface in this style; use it only where described below.
2. A screenshot of the current app mid-walk. Use it for layout and content only, not for style.

THE MAP (the same in every direction): a real photographic satellite image seen straight down, exactly like Apple Maps satellite view, of Geographe, a coastal suburb of Busselton in Western Australia. Single-storey houses with terracotta and grey roofs, backyards with a few pools, peppermint and gum trees, curving streets and cul-de-sacs, and a canal with moored boats and small jetties across the lower right. Flat aerial photography with no 3D buildings, never illustrated, never a cartoon. Zoomed in to street level, about five blocks across the screen.

The suburb is divided into a grid of hexagonal tiles, each about 87 metres across (roughly a quarter of the screen width). We are mid-walk. A winding band of tiles, two or three tiles wide, running from the lower left up to the centre along the streets the walker has followed, has been cleared and shows the sunlit satellite photo. Everything else is hidden as described below. The streets underneath the hidden area are shown as fine dotted lines, so you can still plan a route. A short orange route line follows a street through the cleared tiles and ends at the walker in the middle of the screen: an orange dot with a white ring and a soft glow. On one cleared tile near the route sits one small secret sticker from the sheet (the brass sundial with a startled lizard), about the size of a button, as a secret just found.

STYLE FOR THIS DIRECTION: "Frosted tiles", with no clouds at all. Every unwalked tile is its own soft frosted hexagon, like a tile of sandblasted glass or smooth sugar icing: slightly raised and domed, with softly rounded edges, catching soft light from the top left and casting a gentle shadow onto the map. Neighbouring frosted tiles sit snugly side by side with hairline gaps between them, forming a honeycomb that covers all the unwalked land. The satellite photo is faintly visible through the frosted glass as a heavy blur, so the land feels hidden but present. The streets beneath show as fine white dotted lines across the frosted tiles. Walked tiles have no glass at all: the photo is crisp and sunlit. At the edge of the walked band, near the walker, two freshly walked tiles are mid-dissolve, their glass fading to clear. The interface matches: cool white frosted glass cards with crisp hairline edges, like visionOS, with clean SF Pro type. The buttons are frosted glass hexagons with thin dark line icons. The progress indicator is a thin bar ending in a small hexagon. The "End walk" button is a solid orange capsule with white text. Calm, cool and precise, with orange as the only accent colour.

- A bottom panel with three stats in a row: "24:10" above the label "Time", "1.8 km" above "Distance", and "14" above "New tiles" (the 14 in orange), with a wide "End walk" button underneath.
