Concept art for "Walkies", an iPhone app that turns dog walks into exploring your own suburb. The hidden parts of the suburb are revealed tile by tile as you walk. This image is one of several style directions, all showing the same screen so they can be compared. The aim is calm and refined, not cartoony.

Attached images:
1. A sheet of the app's collectible secret stickers. This sticker art is only for secrets (bits of local history found on walks). Do not draw the interface in this style; use it only where described below.
2. A screenshot of the current app mid-walk. Use it for layout and content only, not for style.
3. An earlier concept where a continuous soft cloud blanket covers unwalked land and its edge steps along a hidden hexagon grid like a honeycomb. Use it for the shape of the cloud edge only, not for the map or the interface.

THE MAP (the same in every direction): a real photographic satellite image seen straight down, exactly like Apple Maps satellite view, of Geographe, a coastal suburb of Busselton in Western Australia. Single-storey houses with terracotta and grey roofs, backyards with a few pools, peppermint and gum trees, curving streets and cul-de-sacs, and a canal with moored boats and small jetties across the lower right. Flat aerial photography with no 3D buildings, never illustrated, never a cartoon. Zoomed in to street level, about five blocks across the screen.

The suburb is divided into a grid of hexagonal tiles, each about 87 metres across (roughly a quarter of the screen width). We are mid-walk. A winding band of tiles, two or three tiles wide, running from the lower left up to the centre along the streets the walker has followed, has been cleared and shows the sunlit satellite photo. Everything else is hidden as described below. The streets underneath the hidden area are shown as fine dotted lines, so you can still plan a route. A short orange route line follows a street through the cleared tiles and ends at the walker in the middle of the screen: an orange dot with a white ring and a soft glow. On one cleared tile near the route sits one small secret sticker from the sheet (the brass sundial with a startled lizard), about the size of a button, as a secret just found.

STYLE FOR THIS DIRECTION: "Sticker-lite": the current app (attached image 2) with a few light sticker touches. The hidden area is the current app's soft, puffy white cloud blanket as in image 2, with the streets beneath drawn across it as clearly visible blue-grey dotted lines, but the cloud edge steps along the hexagon grid like a honeycomb (see attached image 3). The place card and the bottom panel look like die-cut stickers: each has a clearly visible thick white border around a warm cream inside, separated by a thin pale line, and a soft drop shadow lifting it off the map. No dark outlines. Type is SF Pro Rounded in warm near-black, with bold rounded numbers. The four buttons are hexagon-shaped badges (six-sided, not squares and not circles), each with a white sticker border, a cream inside and a small full-colour illustrated icon drawn in the sticker sheet's style with flat colour and soft cel shading: a little brass gear, a red-brown leather book, a folded paper map in green and blue, and a teal location arrow. The progress bar is a thin rounded orange bar. The "End walk" button is a rounded warm orange-red pill with white text and a thin white sticker border. Tidy and calm: the illustrated icons and white borders are the only playful touches.

THE INTERFACE (the same layout and text in every direction):
- A minimal iOS status bar at the very top showing "9:41".
- A place card across the top: "Geographe" with a small down chevron, below it "24 of 460 tiles", on the right a large "5.2%", and a thin progress indicator about 5% full.
- Icon-only buttons with no labels: a gear and a book stacked at the top right just below the place card, and a folded map and a location arrow side by side at the bottom right just above the bottom panel.
- A bottom panel with three stats in a row: "24:10" above the label "Time", "1.8 km" above "Distance", and "14" above "New tiles" (the 14 in orange), with a wide "End walk" button underneath.

Spell every piece of text exactly as written above and add no other text anywhere: no street names, no map labels, no captions, no titles, no logos, no watermark. Portrait iPhone screen, full bleed, no device frame.
